# Change Control Procedure

*Synthetic example document, invented for this repository's evaluation. It describes no real organisation.*

## 1. When this applies

Any change to the approved scope, budget or schedule of a project needs a change request, called a CR. Work on the change must not start until the CR is approved.

## 2. Approval tiers

Tier 1 covers a change of up to $25,000 with a delay of no more than 5 working days. The Project Manager approves it. Tier 2 covers a change from $25,001 to $250,000, or a delay of 6 to 20 working days. The Change Board approves it. Tier 3 covers a change above $250,000, a delay of more than 20 working days, or any change to an approved benefit. The Programme Board approves it.

## 3. The splitting rule

To stop a large change being cut into small ones, all changes to the same work package are added together over a rolling window of 90 days when the tier is decided.

## 4. What a CR must contain

A CR names the work package, the reason for the change and the cost and schedule effect. It must set out at least 2 options and also the option of doing nothing. The impact assessment is completed within 5 working days of submission and the decision is made within 10 working days of submission.

## 5. The Change Board

The Change Board meets every second Tuesday. An urgent CR may be decided by email if 2 board members approve it, and the decision is ratified at the next meeting.

## 6. Risk and change

A CR that adds or alters a risk needs an updated risk register entry before it can be approved. A CR that touches a critical risk is never decided at Tier 1 and goes to Tier 2 at the lowest.

## 7. Emergency changes

If waiting would cause harm to people or damage to the site, the Project Manager may act at once. A retrospective CR must be raised within 48 hours of the action.

## 8. After approval

An approved change is added to the change log and the baseline is reissued within 5 working days. Baselines are numbered B1, B2 and so on. A rejected CR stays in the change log for the life of the programme. Any approved cost change that uses contingency is shown as a separate drawdown line in the next monthly cost report.
